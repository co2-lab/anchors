# v0.2.15

- **Heavy runs wait for one another by what they cost.** Heavy runs of one project, or of several worktrees of it, fought over one machine: a project reported two worktrees' suites at once at a load of 30 to 40 on 10 cores, and timing tests breaking under it. A new `load:` block says what each kind of run costs, in real resources, the same on every machine: levels (`light` 1 CPU / 512 MB, `medium` 2 CPU / 2 GB, `heavy` 4 CPU / 6 GB by default), the tasks `test`, `mutation` and `gate` by file scope, a wait limit per task, and `ci` (CI skips the queue unless asked). A run starts when its cost fits in what the machine has free — its CPUs and memory minus a reserve left to whoever uses it —, through one queue shared by every worktree and project on the machine. A run costlier than the machine runs alone; a run that waited past its limit stops new runs until it fits; a crashed run's lease is reclaimed. The suites of `anchors test` and `anchors mutation` hold their lease through the run and its ingestion, and a tool over the whole project or a batch holds one while it runs. The commit hook jumps the queue. A project without `load:` sees no change. (LDCFS, LDQUS, LDQRS, GTENG)
- **`anchors load`** shows the machine's capacity, the runs holding it and the runs waiting, and which waited past their limit. `$ANCHORS_LOAD_CAPACITY=cpu=8,memory=16GB` sets a machine's capacity, and `$ANCHORS_LOAD=off` turns the queue off. (LDCMS)
- **Two timing tests stop failing under load.** `TestBudgetDeadlineStopsTheGroup` waits for the shell's trap before asking whether the TERM reached it, and `TestRun_sideBySideAndSequential` waits 5 s, not 2, for the other tool to start: under a whole check, starting a process took longer.

To use `load:`, install this binary first: an older one refuses the key.
